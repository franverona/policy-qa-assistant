# Claims Escalation and Authority Limits

## Purpose

This page explains when a support agent can resolve a claims question directly and when it must be escalated. It applies to all Harborline Mutual personal lines products. Last reviewed by the Claims Operations team in March 2024.

## Agent authority limits

Tier 1 support agents may explain coverage, take first notice of loss and give claim status updates. They may not confirm that a loss is covered or quote a settlement amount.

- Tier 1 agents: no payment authority.
- Tier 2 agents: may approve emergency living expense advances up to $1,500.
- Claims adjusters: may settle claims up to $25,000.
- Senior adjusters: may settle claims up to $100,000.
- Claims above $100,000 require approval from the Regional Claims Manager.

## When to escalate

Escalate to a claims adjuster through the CLM queue when any of the following apply:

- The customer disputes a coverage decision or a settlement amount.
- The customer mentions a lawyer, public adjuster or legal action.
- The loss involves injury to a person.
- The customer asks for an appraisal under the policy's appraisal condition.
- The loss was reported more than 60 days after the date of loss.

## Urgent escalations

Some situations need a same-day response. Mark the ticket as P1 and call the duty adjuster on extension 4410 when:

- The home is uninhabitable and the customer has nowhere to stay tonight.
- There is an active water leak, fire damage or a structural safety concern.
- The customer is elderly, disabled or otherwise vulnerable and the loss affects their safety.

## Response times

The claims team aims to contact the customer within 1 business day for standard escalations and within 2 hours for P1 escalations. If the customer has not been contacted within these times, a Tier 2 agent should chase the duty adjuster and note the follow-up in the ticket.

## Complaints

If the customer wants to make a formal complaint, log it in the complaints register within 1 business day. Do not try to resolve a formal complaint yourself. The complaints team sends a written acknowledgement within 5 business days and a final response within 30 days.

## What not to say

Never tell a customer that a claim "will be covered" or "should be fine" before an adjuster has reviewed it. Say instead that the adjuster will review the claim against the policy wording and confirm in writing.
