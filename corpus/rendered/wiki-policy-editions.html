Which Policy Edition Applies

Why this matters

Some products have two editions in force at the same time, with different limits, deductibles and deadlines. Always answer from the edition the customer actually holds. Quoting the wrong edition is one of the most common causes of complaints.

How to find the edition

Open the customer's current declarations page and read the "Policy form" line. HM-HO-2022 and HM-HO-2024 are both HomeShield, but they are different contracts. Never assume the current edition from the product name alone.

HomeShield transition

New HomeShield policies written on or after January 1, 2024 use HM-HO-2024. Existing HM-HO-2022 policies move to HM-HO-2024 at their first renewal on or after July 1, 2024. A loss is always handled under the edition in force on the date of loss, even if the policy has renewed onto the new edition since.

Key HomeShield differences

  • All-peril deductible default: $500 (2022) vs $1,000 (2024).
  • Windstorm and hail deductible: 1% of Coverage A, minimum $500 (2022) vs 2%, minimum $1,000 (2024).
  • Continuous seepage exclusion: 30 days (2022) vs 14 days (2024).
  • Loss of use: up to 24 months (2022) vs 12 months (2024).
  • Theft of jewelry limit: $1,500 (2022) vs $2,000 (2024).
  • Notice of loss: "as soon as practical" (2022) vs no later than 60 days (2024).
  • Suit against us: within one year (2022) vs two years (2024).
  • Water backup endorsement HM-END-04: not available on 2022.

DriveWell transition

DriveWell auto policies move from HM-PA-2023 to HM-PA-2024 at their first renewal on or after January 1, 2024. The main differences are rideshare Period 1 coverage (2024 only), rental car coverage of 45 days instead of 30, no deductible for glass repair (2024 only), and a total loss threshold of 75% instead of 80%.
