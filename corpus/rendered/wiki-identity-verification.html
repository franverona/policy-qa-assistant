Identity Verification and Authorised Callers

Why we verify

Policies contain personal and financial data. Before discussing any policy details, agents must verify that they are speaking to someone allowed to hear them.

Verification questions

Ask the caller for three of the following, and all three must match the policy record:

  • Full name of the named insured.
  • Policy number.
  • Date of birth of the named insured.
  • Mailing address, including postcode.
  • Last four digits of the bank account or card used for payment.

Never ask for a full card number, a password or a full social security number.

Who can discuss the policy

The named insured and any additional named insured on the declarations page can discuss and change the policy. A spouse or partner who is not named can get general information and report a claim, but cannot make changes or cancel the policy.

Authorised third parties

A named insured can add an authorised third party, such as an adult child or a carer, by phone after verification. Authorised third parties can discuss the policy and make payments, but cannot cancel it or change the named insured. Authorisation lasts until the named insured removes it.

Power of attorney and estates

If a caller says they hold a power of attorney, or are acting for a deceased customer's estate, ask them to email a copy of the document to the customer records team. Do not discuss the policy until records confirms it, which normally takes 2 business days.

Failed verification

If the caller fails verification, you may give general product information only. After three failed attempts on the same policy in 24 hours, the policy is flagged and Tier 2 must handle the next contact.
