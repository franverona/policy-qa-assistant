Supporting Vulnerable Customers

Who may be vulnerable

A customer may be vulnerable because of age, illness, disability, bereavement, financial difficulty, domestic abuse or limited English. Vulnerability can be temporary, for example straight after a fire or a death in the family. You do not need proof to treat someone as vulnerable.

Recording a vulnerability

With the customer's consent, add a vulnerability flag to their record and a short note of what support they need, such as "prefers calls after 2 p.m." or "needs large-print letters". Do not record medical details beyond what is needed. Flags are reviewed every 12 months.

Adjustments we can offer

  • Large-print, Braille or audio versions of policy documents, sent within 10 business days.
  • A free interpreter on calls, booked through the language line.
  • A named Tier 2 contact for the life of a claim.
  • Extra time to send documents, up to 30 days beyond any deadline set by us (not deadlines in the policy itself).
  • Payment holidays of up to 2 months during financial hardship, approved by Tier 2.

Bereavement

When a named insured dies, the policy stays in force until the next renewal or until the estate cancels it. Do not cancel the policy or stop autopay just because someone reports the death; ask them to contact the customer records team, as described on the Identity Verification page.

Safety concerns

If a vulnerable customer's loss affects their safety, treat it as a P1 escalation, as described on the Claims Escalation page. If you believe someone is in immediate danger, tell them to call emergency services and inform your team leader.
