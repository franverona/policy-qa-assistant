Taking First Notice of Loss

Purpose

First notice of loss (FNOL) is the first call where a customer reports a claim. A complete FNOL lets the adjuster contact the customer without asking the same questions again. Any agent, including Tier 1, can take FNOL.

Before you start

Verify the caller's identity as described on the Identity Verification page. Check that the policy was in force on the date of loss and note the policy form code from the declarations page.

Information to collect

  • Date, time and location of the loss.
  • What happened, in the customer's own words.
  • Type of damage and which property or vehicle is affected.
  • Whether anyone was injured.
  • Police report number for theft, vandalism or road accidents.
  • Whether the home is still habitable, or the vehicle is still drivable.
  • Best phone number and times to call back.

Claim numbers

The system creates a claim number in the format CLM-YYYY-NNNNNN when FNOL is saved, for example CLM-2024-004512. Read it back to the customer and send it by SMS or email.

Late reports

If the loss is being reported more than 60 days after it happened, take the FNOL as normal and flag it for escalation, as required by the Claims Escalation page. Do not tell the customer the claim will be refused for being late; the adjuster decides.

Mitigation advice

Tell the customer to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, such as turning off the water supply or covering a broken window, and to keep receipts. Reasonable mitigation costs are part of the claim. Do not authorise contractors on the customer's behalf.

What happens next

An adjuster contacts the customer within 1 business day of FNOL, or within 2 hours for P1 claims. Pet and travel claims are not assigned an adjuster; they are handled by the claims processing team from the submitted documents.
